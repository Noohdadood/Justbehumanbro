How it works

Structure first, then the line edit

InYour draft

A story, an article, an essay, a cover letter.

Step 1 · storieshuman-narrative-craft

Audits 30+ structure defaults: stated themes, no subplots, tidy inward endings, linear time.

Step 1 · nonfictionhuman-nonfiction-voice

Openings, endings, real quotes, “says”, verifiable specifics. Never invents a source.

Step 2 · any genreai-prose-line-edit

LAMP's seven problem categories, line by line. Then grammar density (“-ing” clauses, nominalizations), then flagged vocabulary.

The order matters. StoryScope removed clichés and purple prose from AI stories and found detection barely moved, from 95.5% to 93.9% macro-F1. Polishing sentences can't fix structure, so structure goes first.

Limitations

  • The skills haven't been tested yet against real drafts, with and without the skill.
  • Word lists come from 2024–2025 models (GPT-4o, Llama 3) and will drift.
  • Narrow evidence: stories of about 5,000 words, articles under 1,000 words, LAMP mostly literary fiction.
  • Three of the five sources are preprints.
  • The papers disagree on contractions and downtoners, so the skills make no rule about either.

Responsible use

  • For better writing, not for hiding authorship. Disclose AI help where coursework, contests, journalism or publishers require it.
  • The nonfiction skill forbids invented quotes, people, statistics and sources, and leaves [QUOTE NEEDED] instead.
  • In the study, Pangram still caught 99.3% of “humanized” articles. Trained readers catch most AI text too.
